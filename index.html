The idea

Booking is not one problem. It is seven.

Some things have an API. Some have a website that changes every quarter. Some have a person who answers a phone at eleven in the morning and nothing else. A tool that only knows one of those can only book a slice of your trip.

Readyway tries each in turn and stops at the first that works. A task that reaches the last rung has been genuinely attempted six ways, and arrives with everything the earlier attempts learned attached to it — not as a failure, as a shortlist.

Flights

Bought and read back

Fares held, ticketed, then fetched again from the airline before anyone is told it is done.

Hotels

Refundable by default

It looks for a rate you can walk away from, and asks before committing to one you can't.

Restaurants

Actually telephoned

Where there is no booking system, there is a phone call — and a policy engine deciding what may be agreed to.

Activities

Held at the moment of release

Tickets that go on sale at nine are bought at nine, not whenever someone remembers.

Vendors

Chased over email

It writes, waits days if it has to, reads the reply, and answers it.

Money

Never in a prompt

Card details never reach a language model. Spending limits are enforced in code, outside anything that reasons.

The part nobody advertises

It would rather hand your trip back than lie about it.

An autonomous booking agent that is wrong 5% of the time is not 95% useful. It is unusable, because you now have to check all of it — and checking is the work you were trying to avoid.

So the whole system is built around one rule: an attempt is never a booking. Nothing is marked confirmed until it has been read back from the supplier. If a payment goes through and the confirmation doesn't, it stops and asks a human rather than risking a second charge.

A lane that isn't connected declines. It does not improvise, and it does not guess.

Under it

How a booking is made safe

Every supplier, every lane, and every payment runs the same sequence. It is written once, so a new supplier inherits the care rather than reinventing it.

Quote before commit

The price is known before any money moves. A price that changes between quote and payment stops the booking.

The policy decides, not the model

Times, deposits, cancellation terms and party size are checked by code. The agent reports what it was offered; it does not get a vote.

Pay, then commit

Funds are authorised and held. Only then is the supplier asked to commit, with an idempotency key so a retry cannot buy twice.

Read it back

The booking is fetched fresh from the supplier and compared with what was asked for. A mismatch is a failure, however encouraging the response looked.

Unwind cleanly

If anything downstream fails, the hold is cancelled and the money refunded — and the refund is recorded, not assumed.

Wired in

Real systems, not a demo

Readyway holds accounts with the systems that actually take bookings, and speaks each one's protocol properly — signatures, idempotency keys, two-phase commits and all.
