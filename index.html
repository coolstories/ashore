The mechanism

Booking is not one problem. It is seven.

Some venues have an API. Some have a site that changes every quarter. Some have a person who answers a phone at eleven in the morning and nothing else. A system that knows one of those can book a fraction of your trip.

Ashore attempts each in order and stops at the first that works. A task reaching the last lane has been genuinely tried six ways, and arrives carrying everything the earlier attempts learned — not as a failure, as a shortlist.

Air

Bought, then read back

Fares held, ticketed, then fetched again from the airline before anyone is told it is done.

Rooms

Refundable by default

It hunts for a rate you can walk away from, and asks before committing to one you cannot.

Tables

Actually telephoned

Where no booking system exists, there is a phone call — with a policy engine deciding what may be agreed to.

Tickets

Bought at the release

Tickets that go on sale at nine are bought at nine, not whenever somebody remembers.

Vendors

Chased over email

It writes, waits days if it must, reads the reply, and answers it.

Money

Never in a prompt

Card details never reach a language model. Limits are enforced in code, outside anything that reasons.

The part nobody advertises

It would rather hand your trip back than lie about it.

An autonomous agent that is wrong five percent of the time is not ninety-five percent useful. It is unusable — because now you have to check all of it, and checking was the work you were trying to avoid.

So everything here is built on one rule: an attempt is never a booking. Nothing is marked confirmed until it has been read back from the supplier. If money moves and the confirmation does not arrive, it stops and asks a human rather than risk buying twice.

A lane that is not connected declines. It does not improvise.

Under it

How a booking is made safe

Every supplier, lane and payment runs the same sequence. Written once, so a new provider inherits the care instead of reinventing it.

Quote before commit

The price is known before money moves. A price that shifts between quote and payment stops the booking.

The policy decides, not the model

Times, deposits, cancellation terms and party size are checked in code. The agent reports what it was offered; it does not get a vote.

Pay, then commit

Funds authorised and held. Only then is the supplier asked to commit, with an idempotency key so a retry cannot buy twice.

Read it back

The booking is fetched fresh from the supplier and compared against what was asked for. A mismatch is a failure, however encouraging the response looked.

Unwind cleanly

If anything downstream fails, the hold is cancelled and the money refunded — and the refund is recorded, not assumed.

Wired in

Real systems, not a demo

Ashore holds accounts with the systems that actually take bookings, and speaks each one's protocol properly — signatures, idempotency keys, two-phase commits and all.
